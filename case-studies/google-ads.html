02

Starting point and business problem

RevFlow was modeled as a B2B SaaS company using Google Search to acquire demand. The starting account produced 200 leads from a $20,000 monthly budget, but only 30 were considered ICP-fit and sales-accepted.

The commercial issue was lead quality. Google Ads counted every successful demo form submission as a conversion, while sales evaluated leads against ICP fit and buying relevance. The campaign therefore had a large gap between platform-reported conversions and meaningful sales conversions.

StageWho defined it and how
Form submission (lead)Google Ads counted every successful Demo Form Submitted as a conversion: 200 leads.

The account needed a clearer connection between paid-search activity, CRM quality and the downstream sales pipeline.

03

What I identified

  • The campaign was being optimized around Demo Form Submitted rather than lead quality.
  • Every form submission counted equally, even when the lead was outside the ICP or showed weak buying intent.
  • CRM feedback showed that only 15% of leads were ICP-fit and sales-accepted.
  • The existing ad messaging used broad language such as “businesses of all sizes” and “flexible solutions for growing businesses,” while the target market was narrower.

Key finding: Google Ads was being optimized around the form submission, not around the quality of the lead.

04

What I changed

The strategy centered on aligning the paid-search message with the business problem revealed by CRM feedback: teams receiving plenty of MQLs but struggling to identify the opportunities worth pursuing.

DecisionReasonExpected effect
Reframe the conversion problemLead volume alone was not representing business value.Shift evaluation toward qualified, ICP-fit leads and downstream opportunity quality.
Narrow the messageThe existing copy appealed broadly to businesses of different sizes.Speak directly to the buyer problem revealed by the CRM data.
Connect messaging to product valueThe product was described through broad benefits.Position RevFlow around organizing, identifying and prioritizing opportunities.

Execution

  • Reviewed the starting funnel across clicks, leads, qualified leads, opportunities and customers.
  • Examined CRM feedback to classify why leads were being rejected.
  • Identified the mismatch between the Demo Form Submitted conversion and the sales-accepted lead definition.
  • Reviewed existing ad language against the actual ICP and buyer problem.
  • Developed revised Google Search ad messaging around opportunity identification and prioritization.

Why it is stronger: the earlier copy used broad language aimed at businesses of all sizes. The revised copy names the problem the CRM data revealed, plenty of MQLs with unclear opportunities, and positions RevFlow around organizing, identifying and prioritizing opportunities.

06

KPI and measurement thinking

The primary conversion was Demo Form Submitted. No CRM feedback was being sent back to Google Ads about lead quality. That created an optimization signal centered on form submissions rather than the quality of people submitting them. The platform was seeing 200 conversions, while the sales process recognized 30 meaningful conversions.

Primary KPI: ICP-fit and sales-accepted leads, the lead quality that sales considered meaningful. Secondary KPIs: clicks, total leads, opportunities, customers and the CRM reasons behind lead rejection.

Clicks→Leads→ICP-fit / Sales Accepted→Opportunities→Customers→Pipeline
Campaign activityMarketing KPIBusiness implication
Google Search generated form submissionsCRM classified lead qualitySales-accepted leads indicated whether the acquisition signal was aligned with the actual buyer.

Lead volume alone could not show this gap. Measuring ICP-fit, sales-accepted leads moves the evaluation signal closer to business quality.

07

What I learned

  • A healthy lead count can hide a weak acquisition signal when qualification is disconnected from ad-platform conversions.
  • CRM feedback can reveal where paid-search targeting and messaging are attracting the wrong demand.
  • Ad messaging becomes more useful when it reflects the commercial problem sales is trying to solve.

Why this matters for future campaigns

This project demonstrates the ability to trace a paid-search funnel from platform conversions into CRM outcomes, identify a lead-quality mismatch, and use that diagnosis to improve the message presented to the target buyer.

The work is especially relevant to B2B SaaS campaigns where the distance between a form submission and a qualified opportunity can be significant.
